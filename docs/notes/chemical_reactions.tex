\title{Equilibria with chemical reactions}

\author{David Zwicker}
\affiliation{Max Planck Institute for Dynamics and Self-Organization, Am Fa{\ss}berg 17, 37077 G{\"o}ttingen, Germany}

\maketitle

We consider a system of $\Nc$ components, $X_1, \ldots, X_{\Nc}$, that can undergo $\Nb$ different reactions,
\begin{equation}
    \sum_{i=1}^{\Nc} \sigma^{(b)}_{\rightarrow, i} X_i
    \rightleftharpoons
    \sum_{i=1}^{\Nc} \sigma^{(b)}_{\leftarrow, i} X_i
\end{equation}
for each reaction $b=1,\ldots, \Nb$.
Equilibrium then implies that the respective chemical potentials are balanced,
\begin{equation}
    \sum_{i=1}^{\Nc} \sigma^{(b)}_i \mu_i = 0
\end{equation}
where we introduced the stoichiometry matrix $\sigma^{(b)}_i = \sigma^{(b)}_{\leftarrow, i}-\sigma^{(b)}_{\rightarrow, i}$.

To describe this, we notice that each reaction (in general) introduces a constraint,
\begin{equation}
    \sum_{i=1}^{\Nc} q^{(b)}_i \phi_i = \psi_b
\end{equation}
where $q^{(b)}_i$ are linearly independent vectors in the cokernel of the stoichiometric matrix,
\begin{equation}
    \sum_{i=1}^{\Nc} q^{(b)}_i \sigma^{(b)}_i = 0
    \;.
\end{equation}
Once we determined a basis for the cokernel, e.g., using \texttt{scipy.linalg.null\_space}, we can use it to express a linear constraint.
Moreover, we need to impose a grandcanonical potential for the respective species.
The the imposed chemical potential difference relates to the internal energy difference
of the species, which is not part of the free energy in our formulation.
